High school **maker portfolio** work for **MIT application**: stop-motion by carving **one block of wood** per animation. Subtractive process—carving removes material to reveal form—so making the film also destroys the sculpture. By the last frame only the frame sequence and video remain; emphasis stays on process over keeping a physical object.

Inspired by Nelson Goodman's _Ways of Worldmaking_ and “deletion” as how worlds in stories get defined. Sculpture that cuts material away lined up with that idea; each film turned reading and research into something tangible.

## Process

- Turntable, lamp, camera jig, thermal panels to kill stray daylight
- Sessions roughly ~5 hours; blacked-out room, relight and realign as needed
- Match last frame from prior session (anywhere from a few minutes to ~1.5 hr)
- Carve small shavings, reposition, shoot—on the order of **1400+ frames** per piece
- Tools: Dremel and bits, carving knives

Solo work; first carving at a 4th-grade bushcraft camp, then self-directed practice and these projects. Carving also became a slow, focused habit—totems, rings, other small pieces.

## Moby Dick

Reading of how the novel breaks down **land-anchored** order into **life on the Pequod**. Globe tracks Nantucket toward open ocean; pedestal warps into Moby Dick; world tightens into a whaleboat in the jaws; whale becomes whirlpool. Core idea: Ishmael keeps carving away shore-world assumptions until the sea-world is all that’s left.

## Holocaust

Motifs: child in locker (out of sight, in mind); tank on anvils (war held up by industry); melting tank; figure of a pregnant woman (generations that never were); empty chair; grave marked **6,000,000**. Influences included _Life is Beautiful_, Frankl’s _Man’s Search for Meaning_, and Beksiński’s imagery.

## US History

Stack builds from a slave ship through waves and cannon (founding documents as shot), scaffolding for law, industry, and democratic ideas, raised fists, flag and eagle, closing in a single monolith. **Twist:** on screen history runs forward, but carving sometimes stepped **backward** in the block so “earlier” history sat in deeper wood.
